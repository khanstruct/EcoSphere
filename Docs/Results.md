# Results and completed worlds

Completed System worlds now reopen their existing Results directly, bypassing preparation and gameplay. Reopening has no discovery overlay or discovery voice and does not award rewards again. The original completed-world appearance is retained; duplicate completion calls cannot replace it. During development, all progress is session-only by default; the future disk-persistence option in the [world catalog](Worlds.md) is disabled.

Successful First Light clears now lead to Results after all queued announcements, including World Complete, finish actual playback. Losses do not award discoveries or completed worlds. The current Stone tutorial temporarily uses inclusion/excess thresholds of 1,000,000 / 3,000,000 to keep threshold-based rewards out of this demonstration; match and breakaway scoring rates are unchanged. This remains the existing level, intended to become tutorial two when the introductory half-Ice/half-Sunlight level is built later.

Results uses the existing Outfit font, gold frames, heading ornaments, and Celestial buttons. The former world-image frame is replaced by a full rotating globe directly over the starfield, with no frame or backing. It uses the shared planet material, saved sea level and initial rotation on both first completion and historical results. Its square bounds adapt to portrait and landscape layouts; the shared preview remains 256 x 256. The description frame shows the final saved average temperature in Fahrenheit, using the same rounded formatter as gameplay. The collection frame beneath the description receives the newly discovered spheres.

The modal supports zero to three discoveries. With discoveries, it shows rotating element spheres with names beside them and plays `Audio/Voice/element-discovered.mp3` once on entry. Ok disables itself and animates the same sphere previews down to 100-unit collection slots over 0.8 seconds. Continue stays disabled until the animation finishes. Without discoveries, the modal and discovery sound are skipped and Continue is immediately available. UI button-click audio is reused.

Natural discoveries are authored planet placements, awarded only when released during a successful level and not already owned. Recipe and natural discoveries use the same ownership service, including DEV availability. Completion and discovery history live under each planet in the active PlayerProfile. Persistence remains disabled for development; see Architecture.md and Autosave.md.

Continue returns to the completed world's system. Completed planet buttons display a rotating sphere using the same barren-planet material, heightmap, and saved sea level as gameplay. Uncompleted slots remain empty rings. Completed worlds, unlocks, appearance, and the current system persist between scenes only; they reset on a new game session. See Autosave.md. Completed planet clicks open their existing results; unfinished planets open preparation.

`ResultsTools.Build` creates Results, registers it in build settings, assigns the completed-world material to System, and raises First Light's temporary thresholds without rebuilding either existing scene. TestLevelTools/SystemSceneTools preserve these assignments on future rebuilds.

Validation: `ResultsTools.Smoke -resultsSmoke` tests the normal win transition, Stone-only discovery, one discovery voice, Ok click and animation, Continue gating, correct system/planet completion, matching planet material/sea level, rotating map preview, repeat unlock suppression, and zero/one/three discovery cases. It renders portrait, tablet, and wide results previews. All progression is currently memory-only, including normal gameplay. Tutorial and gameplay regression checks also run separately.

Completed worlds retain their original discovered sphere IDs. Revisiting results displays these in the discovered collection immediately, without granting unlocks again or replaying the discovery popup, sound, or flight animation. Pending new rewards remain separate from this historical display. History is serialized with the world for future persistent saves; development sessions still reset on exit. Older saves without history display an empty collection because their original discovery location cannot be recovered.

World-authored discovery tutorial messages can appear after the discovery prompt and collection animation finish. Genesis Alpha uses this to explain the Fire/Void unlock; acknowledging it queues the authored next-world pointer for the return to System. A completed world records both acknowledgments; historical results never repeat the new-discovery lesson.

## Recipe discovery reveal

New recipe awards retain a DiscoveryRecord with output sphere ID, kind, and both ingredient ElementIds. These records are stored with CompletedWorld alongside the existing discovery-ID history; results never infer ingredients from the current recipe book. Old histories without records still display their collected spheres. Naturally occurring discoveries remain distinguished from recipes.

If a fresh results screen includes recipes, discoveries are presented sequentially. Recipe ingredients appear side by side, accelerate together, whiten, become one white shaking orb, then emit a procedural white collision flare and short screen wash. The new sphere appears as the flare fades. Continue is enabled after a short reveal hold and flies that sphere into its collection slot before the next discovery begins. Self-recipes display two separate copies of the same ingredient. Natural-only results retain their existing prompt/collection flow; a natural discovery in a mixed sequence gets a simple reveal without the fusion effect.

The existing discovery sound plays once at each reveal (after the complete combination animation for a recipe). Main results navigation stays blocked until every result is collected. Genesis Alpha's explanatory tutorial appears only after the complete sequence. Reopening completed results shows historical discoveries directly, with no fusion animation, discovery sound, or new rewards.

Assets: Resources/RecipeRevealStyle.asset supplies the sphere/flare materials and editable hold, merge, volatile, flash, reveal and collection timings, shake amplitude and screen-wash opacity. UIDiscoverySphere.shader handles whitening/glow and a labeled procedural orb fallback for elements without a pure-sphere appearance. UIDiscoveryFlare.shader draws the expanding white flare without image assets. Both support UI masking and alpha. Canonical ingredient art uses a dedicated single-element sphere when one exists; mixed sphere identity is never substituted for an element. All sphere preview render textures remain 256×256, and the white wash uses the existing 64×64 fill.

RecipeRevealTools.Configure builds/validates the two material assets. RecipeRevealTools.Smoke with -recipeRevealSmoke checks an actual natural + pair + self award sequence, ingredient serialization even after the recipe book changes, blocked input during animation, every reveal phase, sequential collection, portrait/landscape renders and historical results without repeated awards. Alpha followup, natural-results and contribution/award regressions also pass. Preview frames are under the validation project's Logs/Recipe-*.png.

Recipe reveal audio timing uses the actual sphere-combine clip length rather than fixed phase seconds. The five combination phase values on RecipeRevealStyle act as relative weights across that duration. The existing element-discovered voice is scheduled on the same DSP clock at the exact end; after-reveal hold and collection still use their own durations. Play-mode checks verify pair/self combine counts, scheduled clip-length handoff, no premature discovery announcement, and unchanged natural/revisited results.
